Disposal leaking or flooding the sink? Cut the power before anything else

A garbage disposal that is leaking into the cabinet or backing water up into the sink is an urgent kitchen problem, and the line for garbage disposal repair is open 24/7. A disposal is a small grinder bolted under the sink drain, driven by an electric motor. While you wait, switch the disposal off at the wall, unplug it from the outlet under the sink if you can do so with dry hands, and stop using the sink.

Never put your hand or a utensil into the disposal, even when it is off, until the plug is out. Turn off the cold water under the sink if the cabinet is wet. If water has reached the outlet or the cord, stay out of the cabinet and shut the kitchen breaker only from a dry spot at the panel.

Move cleaning products out of the cabinet and set a bucket under the leak. Nights, weekends and holidays are handled the same way, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

What the noise or silence is telling you

A hum with no spin means something is jammed against the grinding plate, often a fruit pit, a bottle cap or a spoon that fell in. A loud metallic rattle means a hard object is loose inside the chamber. A disposal that does nothing at all, not even a hum, usually has a tripped reset button on the bottom, a dead outlet, or a motor that has burned out.

A grinding sound with no food in it points at worn bearings or a damaged flywheel. If the motor smells hot or like burning plastic, turn it off and leave it off. Running it longer only makes the repair larger.

The plumber who comes out starts by unplugging the unit, then uses a hex wrench in the center socket at the bottom to turn the plate by hand, which clears most jams. After that comes the reset button, the electrical connection, and a test run with water.

Signs the disposal needs repair, not another try

  • ✓Water under the sink cabinet, or a damp towel that is wet again the next day
  • ✓A hum, a click or a buzz when you flip the switch
  • ✓A reset button that pops out every time you press it back in
  • ✓Slow draining at the sink even with the disposal running
  • ✓A foul smell that does not go away after cleaning the splash guard
  • ✓Water leaking from the dishwasher hose connection at the side of the unit
  • ✓Rust at the housing seam, or drips from the body itself

A leak from the body means the housing has cracked or corroded, and repair is rarely possible. A leak from the top flange or the drain connection often means a worn seal or a loose mounting ring. Those can be repaired or reset, and the plumber tightens and reseals them while the unit is out of its mount.

Common causes of repeat jams and clogs

Most disposals die from what goes into them. Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves wraps around the plate. Grease and oil cool in the pipe past the disposal and harden into a plug. Coffee grounds and eggshell fragments settle into the trap and form a paste.

If your sink backs up even when the disposal works, the clog is in the drain line, not the grinder. A drain cleaning visit clears the line with a cable, and a hydro jetting pass can strip heavy grease from the pipe wall. If the same sink clogs again within weeks, the line may have a belly or a bad connection that needs a camera look.

Running cold water for several seconds before and after you grind helps keep fat moving. Hot water melts grease on the way down, and it can harden again further along the line.

A replacement disposal is sized by the motor, a half horsepower unit for light use and larger for families that cook a lot. Pick a unit with a continuous-feed switch or batch-feed cap based on how you use the sink, and ask whether a new plug or hardwire connection is needed.

Replacing a disposal: what the job involves

Replacing a disposal is more than swapping a box. The plumber disconnects the drain trap, releases the mounting ring, and lowers the old unit. A new sink flange gasket is seated with putty or a seal, the new unit is locked onto the mounting ring, and the dishwasher inlet knockout is opened if a dishwasher connects. Then the discharge elbow and trap go back on, and the unit is tested with water running.

Wiring comes next. Some units plug into a cord under the sink, others are wired to a junction box. Electrical work near water is done with the power off and tested before any wire is touched. If the outlet or switch is faulty, the fix may be an electrician's job, and you will hear that before work begins.

If you are adding a sink, a dishwasher or a new line in a remodel, plumbing installation covers how those planned jobs are handled.

Rental kitchens and shared units

About 62 percent of Loma Linda households rent, so garbage disposals are often a landlord's repair and a tenant's emergency. A tenant who finds water in the cabinet should switch the unit off, photograph the damage, and tell the property manager in writing. A manager who calls the line should give the unit number and say whether the tenant will be home.

In a multi-unit building, a disposal leak can run down into the unit below. Mention that on the call so the plumber knows to check the cabinet and the wall for hidden water. Tenants should not attempt to remove the unit themselves.

If a lease or the building owner has a preferred way of handling repairs, follow it, and keep a copy of any invoice. A repair that is documented gets resolved faster than one that is not.

After-hours and weekend disposal calls

Disposals fail on the busiest nights: after a holiday dinner, on a Sunday with guests, or while someone is washing up late. The line is open 24/7, and a night call gets the same clear price before work starts as a weekday call.

For a leaking unit at night, switching it off and clearing the cabinet is the main step, and the plumber then checks where the water is coming from. If the disposal is jammed, you may be able to wait for a same-day visit. If the sink is backing up, or the water is near the outlet, call right away.
