Drain backing up right now? Do this first

A drain that is overflowing or backing up is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7 for exactly that. Drain cleaning means removing whatever blocks a pipe, such as grease, hair, wipes, paper or roots, so wastewater can flow out of the house again. While you wait, stop using every sink, tub and toilet, turn off the dishwasher and washer, and keep children and pets out of any wet area.

If water is rising near outlets, appliances or a floor furnace, stay out of the room and switch off power at the panel only if you can reach it with dry hands and dry feet. A plumber will ask which fixtures are affected, because that tells us whether the clog sits in a single branch or in the line that serves the whole house.

Nights, weekends and holidays are normal hours for this work. A tub that fills at 11 p.m. does not wait politely for Monday, and same-day appointments are offered when they are available.

Skip these moves while you wait

  • Do not pour chemical drain opener into standing water. It sits in the pipe, burns skin on contact and can splash back when a plumber opens the line.
  • Do not keep flushing to see if it clears. Every flush sends more water toward the lowest open drain, often a shower or tub.
  • Do not run a rented auger past the first bend if you feel springy resistance. That can be a cracked section, and forcing a cable through it makes the damage worse.
  • Do not mix cleaners. Bleach and acid products together release fumes that are dangerous in a small bathroom.

Reading the signs before the drain stops completely

Most clogs announce themselves for days or weeks. Catching them early often means a shorter visit and less cleanup.

  • ✓Water drains slowly from one sink or tub, then slowly from a second fixture.
  • ✓A gurgle comes from a nearby drain when you flush or run the washer.
  • ✓Standing water collects in the shower while the toilet is flushing.
  • ✓A sewage smell rises from a floor drain, a tub or the cleanout in the yard.
  • ✓Water in the lowest tub or shower rises when the washer drains.
  • ✓The same fixture clogs again within a few weeks of being cleared.

One slow fixture usually points to a branch line. Two or three fixtures going slow together, especially the ones lowest in the house, point to the main drain.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, scraps and the disposal

The kitchen sink is the single most common stopped drain, and the cause is rarely the thing you saw go down last. Cooking fat runs through the trap hot and liquid, then cools on the pipe wall into a waxy coat. Coffee grounds, rice and pasta stick to that coat, and within a few months the opening has shrunk to a fraction of its size.

A double sink with a disposal adds a second trouble spot. The disposal grinds food fine, but fine particles still settle in the horizontal run behind the wall, where the pipe slopes gently toward the main. If the sink backs up into the other basin, the blockage is past the tailpiece and trap, and a hand plunger will usually only move it around.

Clearing a kitchen line means cabling past the trap arm and into the wall, then flushing the line with water until it runs clean. When the grease coat is thick, a hydro jetting pass cuts it off the pipe wall instead of just punching a hole through it. A broken garbage disposal that jams and drains slowly is a separate repair, and the plumber can tell you which problem you have once the sink is open.

Snaking and cabling: what the tools actually do

People use snaking and cabling as the same word, but the tools are different sizes. A hand-held drum auger, often called a snake, is a thin coiled wire that fits through a sink trap or a tub overflow. It reaches a few feet to perhaps a dozen, which is enough for a bathroom lavatory or a tub.

A cable machine is a powered drum with a thicker cable and a set of cutting heads. It is used through a cleanout or a removed toilet to reach a branch line or the main. The head chews through hair, paper, soft roots and sludge, and it leaves a clear channel in the pipe center.

The limit is that a cable opens a hole and does not scrub the whole pipe. If water drains quickly after the visit and then slows over the next month, the blockage was only partly removed. That is the point to ask about a camera look or a jetting pass instead of paying for the same cabling again.

What Loma Linda houses and the city sewer mean for a clog

The median home in Loma Linda was built in 1984, and many tracts have branch lines that are decades old. Older cast iron and clay can develop rough interior walls, and rough walls catch grease and paper sooner than smooth plastic does. If your drain clogs more than twice a year, the pipe itself may be part of the cause.

Where a clog sits also decides who fixes it. The City of Loma Linda owns and runs the sewer collection system, and the wastewater is treated through a joint powers agreement with San Bernardino. Where the city's pipe ends and the owner's begins is worth confirming, so ask Loma Linda Public Works or the plumber who comes out to check where that line falls before you assume a bill.

A rental in Loma Linda adds one more question, which is who calls. If the clog is in a shared line serving several units, tell the property manager as well, because the repair may serve more than one household.

Storm drain and yard drain cleaning

Storm drains and sewer drains are separate systems, and mixing them up is how people end up with wet yards. Roof gutters, patio drains, area drains and catch basins carry rainwater to the street or a dry well, and they should never connect to the sewer line from the toilets.

Loma Linda gets about 12.87 inches of rain in a normal year, measured at the Redlands NOAA station, and most of it falls from January to March. Summer months can drop near 0.1 inch each. That means leaves, dust and mulch collect for months in a patio drain and then wash into it all at once. Clearing a catch basin and flushing the yard line in late autumn is far cheaper than dealing with a flooded patio door in February.

If a storm drain in the street or on public property is blocked, that is Loma Linda Public Works territory, not a job for a home plumber. A plumber can still clear the private branch that connects your downspouts and yard drains to the street. The same goes for homes in nearby towns such as Redlands or Colton, where we also take drain calls.

Which method fits which clog

Drain cleaning methods and when each makes sense

Powered cable machineA branch line or main is blocked with hair, paper or soft rootsOpens a channel and leaves the pipe wall coated

Camera inspection after cleaningA clog returned or the line is oldAdds time, but it shows the cause instead of guessing

After the line runs clear, a sewer camera inspection shows whether the pipe is cracked, offset or sagging. A line that keeps stopping in the same spot is usually telling you something about the pipe, not about what you flushed.

One short note on drain line leaks. A drain that smells or stains a ceiling may be leaking at a joint instead of clogging. A plumber should check it with a camera or a pressure test before any wall is opened.

What decides the price of drain cleaning

Every house is different, so the same clog can take twenty minutes in one home and several hours in the next. These are the factors that move the number.

  • Access. A drain with a cleanout is faster than one that needs a toilet pulled or a trap removed.
  • Where the clog sits. A fixture trap is simple, while a main line near the street takes a longer cable and more time.
  • Pipe material and age. Old cast iron or clay needs gentler tools than plastic.
  • The method chosen. A cable pass, a hydro jetting pass and a camera run are different jobs.
  • After-hours timing. A night, weekend or holiday visit costs more than a weekday appointment.
  • Repeat clogs. A line that returns may need a camera look and a different plan.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental water discharge and exclude gradual leaks and wear, so a slow clog that damages a floor can be argued either way.
