Tank failed and water spreading? Start here

A failed tank is an emergency, and the line for water heater replacement is open 24/7 for nights, weekends and holidays. A replacement means removing the old tank, setting a new one, and reconnecting the water, the fuel and the venting or power. Until help comes, close the cold water valve at the tank, stop using hot water, and keep everyone away from wet floors near outlets.

Shut off the fuel next. On a gas tank, turn the gas control knob to off. On an electric tank, turn off its breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water. If the tank is in a garage with a car or tools nearby, move what you can out of the wet zone and photograph the damage first.

Gas odor changes everything. Get out of the building before doing anything else, phone SoCalGas on 1-800-427-2200 from the yard or street, and use 911 if the gas company does not pick up. A plumber comes after the gas is handled.

Signs a tank has reached the end of its life

  • ✓Water seeping from the seam at the bottom of the tank or from under the outer jacket
  • ✓Rust-colored hot water, while the cold taps run clear
  • ✓A rumbling or popping noise from the tank, which often means hardened sediment
  • ✓A repair history: a second element, a second thermostat or a relief valve replaced within a short time
  • ✓A tank that has stood in the same spot for more than 10 years
  • ✓Rust streaks or flaking at the inlet and outlet fittings

One sign alone does not always mean replacement. A pooling leak from the tank body does, because the steel shell has corroded through and no part can seal it. If your problem looks more like a worn element or a pilot that will not hold, a plain water heater repair may be the better call, and the plumber will say so.

Hot water gone completely: replace or revive?

When the shower runs cold and the tank is not leaking, the cause is often a bad element, thermostat or gas valve, and a repair restores heat. When the tank is old, rusty and has already been repaired once, putting money into parts is a gamble. The plumber checks the basics first and then tells you plainly which side of that line your heater is on.

From old tank to new: how the visit runs

  1. 1The plumber confirms the fuel, size, vent route and location, then closes the water and fuel and drains the old tank.
  2. 2The old unit is disconnected from water, gas or power, and the vent, then hauled out of the closet or garage.
  3. 3The new tank is set on a level pan or platform, and the water lines get new connectors and shutoff valves where needed.
  4. 4A gas unit gets a new gas connector and a leak test of every joint. An electric unit gets a power circuit check.
  5. 5The relief valve and its discharge pipe are set, the tank is filled, air is bled from the hot lines, and the burner or elements are started.
  6. 6Venting, drafting and temperature are tested, and the plumber walks you through the controls.

The tank gets bracing against earthquake movement, and the discharge pipe from the relief valve is run to a safe outdoor or drain point. If the home has a closed plumbing system, with a check valve or backflow device, the plumber also checks whether an expansion tank is needed under the California Plumbing Code.

Gas replacements and the South Coast air rules

Loma Linda falls inside the South Coast Air Quality Management District, SCAQMD. SCAQMD Rule 1121 sets a low-NOx limit for residential gas storage water heaters under 75,000 Btu per hour, which is why replacement tanks sold in this area are the low-NOx models. A plumber buying from a local supplier will be handed compliant units.

In June 2025 the SCAQMD board voted down proposed zero-NOx rules covering water heaters, so for now a gas tank can be swapped for a compliant gas tank. Rules can change, so ask the seller to confirm the exact model meets Rule 1121 before it goes in your garage.

Permits, inspections and who may do the work

The city division to ask is Building and Safety, at (909) 799-2836. It issues plumbing permits, and its published FAQ treats plumbing as a separate permit, yet nothing on its pages names water heaters, so the answer for a tank swap has to come from them. The counter line takes calls from 8 to 10 in the morning, Monday through Thursday.

State law adds a licensing rule. A job that needs a building permit must go to a licensed contractor at any price, and the plumbing license class explicitly includes water-heating equipment. You can look up any contractor on the state license board website before you let them in the house.

Heat, old homes and the closet your tank lives in

Heat is the local stress on a tank. The 1991 to 2020 normals at the Redlands NOAA station list about 25 days a year of 100 degrees or more. A tank in an attic space or a garage works through that heat all summer, and the metal and controls feel it. Only about 3 days a year fall to freezing, so cold damage is rarely what killed your tank.

Roughly 42.5 percent of the homes in the city were built before 1980, by the 2020 to 2024 Census estimates. In those homes the tank often sits in a cramped closet or a garage corner, with old supply lines and corroded shutoffs. When the old tank comes out, the plumber looks at the lines and valves around it, and tells you which ones should be changed now instead of next year.

Choosing the new unit: tank size, fuel type and the alternatives

Most replacements go back in with the same fuel and about the same capacity, because the gas line, the vent and the electrical circuit are already in place. Changing fuel type means new lines, a new circuit or a new vent, and each change adds work and a permit question.

A tank that fails at night or on a weekend

Tanks do not fail at convenient hours. The line is open 24/7, so a flooded closet on a Friday night gets the same answer as a dead heater on a Tuesday morning. After-hours calls get the leak stopped and the area made safe first. If the new unit is not on a truck that night, the plumber can often arrange a same-day appointment as soon as the right model is available.

Households in Redlands or Colton can use the same line, since service reaches past city limits. Holiday weekends are the busiest time for tanks that were already near the end, so a heater showing the signs above is worth replacing before the next long weekend.

What moves the price of a new tank

  • Tank size, fuel type and the efficiency level of the model you choose
  • Whether the old gas line, vent, shutoff valves or electrical circuit can be reused
  • Access to the heater, from a ground-level closet to a high attic platform
  • Permit, bracing, relief piping and expansion tank work that the install calls for
  • After-hours, overnight or weekend work when the tank has already failed

A burst tank is usually a sudden event that homeowner policies tend to cover, while slow seepage and wear tend to be excluded. Keep the failed tank until your insurer's adjuster has seen it, and photograph the wet area.
