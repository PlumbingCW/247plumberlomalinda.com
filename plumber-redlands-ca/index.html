Why Redlands homes built before 1980 set the plumbing agenda

Emergency dispatch for Redlands runs around the clock, nights, weekends and holidays. The base is in Loma Linda, and Redlands sits about 4 miles east of it by straight line. A licensed plumber takes the call, and you hear a clear price before any work starts.

About 65.8 percent of Redlands housing units were built before 1980, according to the city's housing element data drawn from the American Community Survey. About 28.7 percent date from 1959 or earlier, and the 1970s was the biggest building decade. Original supply lines, second-generation water heaters and aging drain pipe are ordinary in that mix. Ask any plumber what pipe material they see before you agree to a repair.

Which jobs older Redlands houses call for first

The order below follows what an older housing stock tends to need. A tank leaking from its body cannot be patched, so water heaters lead. Drains come next, then hidden leaks, then the sewer lateral.

When a tank leaks from the bottom, water heater replacement is the usual answer, because the steel shell has failed. Slow tubs and kitchen sinks in older branch lines often respond to drain cleaning. A stain on a ceiling or a meter that keeps moving calls for leak detection before anyone cuts drywall.

Cold is rarely the cause here. The NOAA station at Redlands, using 1991 to 2020 normals, shows about 3 days a year at or below freezing. A sudden wet ceiling in Redlands is more likely a worn part than a frozen pipe.

Two water supplies feed Redlands taps

The city's Municipal Utilities and Engineering Department reports that about half of its water is treated surface water from the Santa Ana River and Mill Creek watersheds and the State Water Project. The other half comes from local wells in the Bunker Hill Groundwater Basin. The city also disinfects with chlorine, not chloramine.

Hardness is reported separately for each supply in the 2026 water quality report, which uses 2025 data. Groundwater averaged 148 mg/L, with a range of 100 to 200 mg/L. Treated surface water averaged 117 mg/L, with a range of 94 to 140 mg/L. The report gives mg/L only, so test your tap before you spend money on a water softener.

In July 2020 a 16-inch transmission line break near Ford Park left more than 6,000 accounts facing low pressure. If pressure drops on your whole street, call Utilities Customer Service at (909) 798-7516 first. If only your house is affected, the problem is probably on your side.

Sewer backups: the city main, your lateral and the first call

Redlands owns and runs its sewer collection system and the Redlands Wastewater Treatment Facility. The city's page also notes about 20,000 private lateral connections, the pipes that run from each building to the main. When a drain backs up, the city asks you to call (909) 798-7516 on weekdays or (909) 798-7681 after hours.

A city crew checks the main first. If the main is clear, the city may advise calling a plumber for a blockage in the lateral. That is the point where sewer line repair or a camera run enters the picture,, because a backup looks the same from the toilet whatever the cause.

What Redlands requires for water heater and sewer permits

The city's permit handout says a permit is required to add or replace a water heater, to add or replace sewer lines, and for new plumbing work. The list is not exhaustive. Repiping and drain line repair are not named, so call Building and Safety at (909) 798-7536 before assuming either way.

Building and Safety works from the One Stop Permit Center at 35 Cajon Street. California also requires a contractor license on any project that needs a building permit, so ask any plumber for a license number and check it on the CSLB lookup.

Smiley Park, the university and Orange Street

Plenty of Redlands is old on purpose. The A.K. Smiley Public Library on West Vine Street was donated in 1898, Smiley Park holds the Redlands Bowl, and Kimberly Crest House and Gardens on Prospect Drive dates to 1897. The city lists eight local historic districts, among them Eureka Street, West Highland Avenue and Garden Hill.

If your house is in one of those older blocks, say so on the call. Older plumbing is likely, and a camera look before any pipe replacement saves guessing.

Near the University of Redlands on East Colton Avenue, or along Redlands Boulevard, Lugonia Avenue and Orange Street, giving the cross street helps the plumber find you faster.

Before a plumber arrives in Redlands

Call (909) 505-3225 and a licensed plumber answers 24/7. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. While you wait, work through these four steps.

Shut off the main valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. In an older Redlands house the valve may be stiff, so do not force it with a tool.

Kill power near water

If water reaches outlets, a ceiling light or the water heater wiring, switch off power at the panel only if you can reach it on a dry floor.

Clear the area and move valuables

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Keep children and pets out of any room with a backed-up drain.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the street, the cross street, and whether the water looks clean or like sewage. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or 1-800-427-2200 first.

Where in Redlands we work, from 92373 to Mentone

ZIP 92373 carries the civic and institutional addresses of the city, including City Hall on East State Street, the university, the hospital and Esri. The second Redlands ZIP, 92374, is also on our map. We serve both.

The city's water service area reaches beyond its limits. It includes Mentone, a Census-designated place with 9,557 residents in 2020, parts of Crafton Hills and San Timoteo Canyon, and a small part of San Bernardino. We work in Mentone as well.

Neighbors often call us across the line. Colton lies to the west of Loma Linda, and Highland is another town we serve.
