Three plumbing problems Colton keeps producing

Emergency dispatch for Colton runs around the clock. Colton lies west of Loma Linda, a few miles away by straight line, and the problems that bring calls from this city are fairly consistent. A licensed plumber takes each call and you hear a clear price before any work starts.

  • Scale from hard groundwater building up in water heaters, fixtures and aerators
  • Sewer clogs and backups in lateral lines that the property owner has to sort out
  • Aging pipes and tanks in a housing stock that is about half older than 1980

The sections below take them in that order, then cover permits, ground conditions and the places a caller is likely to name.

Hard well water and what it leaves behind

Colton's water comes from groundwater wells, and the city can also draw on a connection with Rialto and treated groundwater from San Bernardino. The 2024 water quality report lists total hardness for the Colton/Rialto basin wells at 150 to 230 mg/L, averaging about 207 mg/L. The Rialto source tested at 160 mg/L.

That is hard enough that scale is a realistic worry. A tank water heater collects it on the bottom, and a failing heater from scale buildup is a job for water heater repair or replacement. Test your tap before choosing a softener.

The city disinfects with chlorine, averaging 1.12 mg/L at the basin wells. It also installed two treatment systems serving three wells affected by perchlorate, and the report says treated water came out below detection. If water looks or tastes wrong, call Colton Water Utilities at (909) 370-6131 before you blame your pipes.

Sewer backups in a city that treats its own sewage

Colton owns and runs its wastewater plant, more than 100 miles of sewer mains and its lift stations. The plant also serves Grand Terrace and nearby unincorporated county areas. Treated effluent moves on to the RIX facility that Colton and San Bernardino own jointly.

The city pages we read do not spell out who owns a private lateral or when a cleanout is required. Call the Wastewater office at (909) 370-5099 before you dig, and let a plumber run a camera down the line first. A clog near the house is a drain cleaning job, while a broken or sagging lateral becomes sewer line repair.

For water emergencies after hours, the city lists (909) 370-5000.

Housing that is about half older than 1980

City housing data from the American Community Survey puts about 52.0 percent of Colton's 16,549 housing units before 1980. The largest single group, 28.5 percent, went up in the 1980s.

The city's history page says railroad workers in south Colton, the area south of I-10, built homes from wood shipping crates. Houses with that kind of past have often been added to many times. Whatever the original build, ask the plumber to show you the pipe in the wall or under the floor before you agree to a replacement.

Ground movement along the San Jacinto fault zone

Colton's 2018 General Plan Safety Element says the San Jacinto fault zone runs through the city. It marks liquefaction-prone areas along the north and south banks of the Santa Ana River and in the Reche Canyon area. It also reports subsidence between 1933 and 1960 of about 2.5 inches in north Colton and about 1 inch in south Colton, which has slowed since.

The element does not tie any of this to pipe failures. Even so, buried lines in ground that moves are worth looking at directly, and a sewer camera inspection shows what the pipe looks like instead of leaving it to guesswork. After any earthquake, if you smell gas, leave and call 911 or 1-800-427-2200 from outside.

The nearest NOAA station is Redlands, about 7 miles east. Its 1991 to 2020 normals show 26 days a year at or above 100 degrees and just 12.87 inches of rain. In a dry year, a yard that stays soggy points to a leak in the pipe, not to weather, and leak detection finds where.

Colton Crossing, the Carnegie Library and Arrowhead Regional Medical Center

Colton is a rail city. The Union Pacific line crosses the BNSF line at Colton Crossing, where a flyover had its ribbon cutting on 28 August 2013. I-10 and I-215 also pass through. When you call, name the nearest cross street or freeway ramp so the plumber can place you.

Other places that callers name include Arrowhead Regional Medical Center at 400 North Pepper Avenue and the 1891 Carnegie Library on La Cadena Drive, now the Colton Area Museum. Colton's electric utility is municipal, so a power question near flooded wiring goes through the city as well.

Before a plumber arrives in Colton

Call (909) 505-3225 and a licensed plumber answers 24/7, with same-day appointments when available. In the meantime, protect the house with these four moves.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the shutoff by the meter or at the house clockwise until it stops. A hard-water valve that has not moved in years can seize, so do not force it.

Kill power near water

If water touches a plug, a light fixture or the water heater circuit, shut the breaker only if you can stand on a dry floor to reach the panel.

Clear the area and move valuables

Move furniture, cardboard and electronics off wet floors, and keep children out of rooms where sewage has risen from a drain.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the plumber the cross street, the water color and where it starts. If you smell gas, leave first and call 911 or 1-800-427-2200.

Where in Colton we work, across 92324 and over the line

Colton uses a single ZIP code in our records, 92324. Arrowhead Regional Medical Center and the Public Works office on South 10th Street both carry it.

Callers often live just beyond it. Bloomington is an unincorporated community, and Rialto is the city whose water system connects to Colton's. We take those calls on the same line.

Two other towns tie into Colton's sewer system. Grand Terrace is served by Colton's plant, and San Bernardino co-owns the RIX facility.
