Water coming through the ceiling of your shop or rental? What to do first

A burst line, a backed up floor drain or an overflowing restroom in a business is an emergency, and the line for commercial plumbing is open 24/7. Commercial plumbing means the same pipes, drains and fixtures as a house, but on larger lines with more fixtures per branch. While you wait, shut off the building main or the branch valve for the affected suite, and stop using restrooms and sinks that drain to the same line.

Tell staff to stay out of any area where water is near outlets, display lighting, cash registers or a panel. If you can reach the electrical panel on dry floor, turn off the circuit for the wet area. Move stock, paper records and electronics off the floor, and photograph the water before it is cleaned up.

Nights, weekends and holidays are treated the same as a weekday. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and after-hours calls get a clear price before work starts.

How commercial plumbing shows up along Barton Road and Mountain View Avenue

Barton Road is Loma Linda's main commercial corridor, with retail centers that include grocery and neighborhood market anchors, and Mountain View Avenue carries the I-10 interchange and city parks. Redlands Boulevard has a medical building near the VA hospital. A strip of small suites on one roof often shares a single water service and a single sewer lateral, so a problem in one unit can affect the next.

That has a practical meaning for you. If your toilet backs up and the neighbor's floor drain bubbles, the blockage is in the shared line, not your fixture. Say that on the call, and ask your landlord which suites share the lateral. If the problem turns out to be on the street side of the meter, the plumber tells you to involve the city instead.

Property managers and landlords: 62 percent of households here rent

About 62 percent of households in Loma Linda are renters, which makes the landlord and property manager a normal caller, not a rare one. Duplexes, four-plexes and small apartment buildings have shared drains, shared water heaters and shared shutoffs, and one failed part can touch several tenants at once.

California Civil Code 1941.1 treats plumbing in good working order, with hot and cold running water at the fixtures, as part of what makes a rental habitable. That is general information, not legal advice. In practice it means a stoppage or a lost hot water supply should be handled the same day when possible, in writing, with photos.

When you call, give the building address, unit numbers, who holds the keys, and whether tenants are home. The plumber can leave a written note of what was found and repaired for your records, and the shutoff locations for each suite are worth labeling on the spot.

A backflow test notice came in: what it is and who may do it

A backflow assembly is a valve device on a water line that keeps used water from flowing backward into the public supply. Restaurants, car washes, medical offices, irrigation systems and fire lines are common places to find one. The water supplier, which here is the City of Loma Linda Water Department, requires testing, and the city has a backflow and cross-connection page on its water and sewer site.

Under the State Water Board's cross-connection rules, which took effect July 1, 2024, an assembly is tested at least once a year and again after it is installed, repaired or moved. The supplier may ask for more frequent tests where the hazard is higher. Only testers holding certification from an organization the State Water Board recognizes may do the test.

If a test fails, the assembly is repaired or replaced and tested again. Ask when you call whether the plumber who comes out holds that tester certification or whether a separate tester is needed. A missed test deadline can cause a notice from the water supplier, so do not wait for the second reminder.

Grease trap and interceptor cleaning for restaurants and food service

A grease trap is a box or tank in the kitchen drain line that cools wastewater and lets fat float to the top before it reaches the sewer. When it is full, grease escapes into the line downstream, hardens, and blocks the pipe. Warning signs include a sour smell near the floor drains, slow sinks, and water that backs up in the dish area after the lunch rush.

Cleaning means removing the lid, pumping out the fat and solids, scraping the walls and baffles, and checking the inlet and outlet. How often depends on volume, so a busy restaurant needs it more often than a small cafe. Your sewer agency or the city sets any requirements. The City of Loma Linda owns the sewer collection system, so ask the city what applies to your address.

Between cleanings, scrape plates into the trash, keep fryer oil out of sinks, and use a sink strainer. If the line downstream keeps blocking, a hydro jetting pass clears the pipe wall of hardened grease, and a sewer line cleaning visit reaches further down the lateral.

Commercial leak detection: what it can and cannot do

Leak detection in a commercial building uses the same tools as a house, but the job is larger. The plumber checks the water meter with every fixture off, closes valves by zone to see which branch is losing water, and uses acoustic and thermal tools to narrow the area. Large slabs, rooftop lines and shared walls take longer than a single bathroom.

Be realistic about the limits. A pipe inside a concrete slab, behind a finished wall or under a parking lot may only be located to within a few feet, and a wall or floor may still need opening to confirm. An honest first visit finds the zone and the likely cause, and a second step repairs it.

For a house or a small unit, the full process is described on the leak detection page.

Sump and ejector pumps in basements and below-grade suites

A sump pump moves groundwater out of a pit, and an ejector pump lifts toilet and sink waste from a below-grade restroom up to the sewer. These pumps matter for basements, parking structures and any suite with a restroom below the main line. A failing pump shows up as a high-water alarm, a flooded pit, or a restroom that will not drain. Check that the float moves freely, and call when the pit keeps filling.

Nights, weekends and the dispatch call for a business

A restaurant that floods during Friday service, a clinic whose restroom backs up on Sunday, or a landlord with a burst line at midnight all call the same number. The line is open 24/7. When you call, say whether the business is open, whether customers are inside, and whether the water has been shut off.

After hours, the first step is stopping the damage, then a plan for the repair. Some jobs can be finished that night. Others are made safe and finished the next day. Either way, you get a clear price before any work starts. Businesses in San Bernardino and Redlands are served from the same line.
