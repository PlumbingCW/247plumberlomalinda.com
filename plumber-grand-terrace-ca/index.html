Grand Terrace through the year: dry summers, a wet winter, rare frost

Emergency dispatch for Grand Terrace runs around the clock, nights, weekends and holidays. The base is in Loma Linda, and Grand Terrace sits about 4 miles west of it by straight line. A licensed plumber takes the call, and you hear a clear price before any work starts.

The nearest long-record NOAA station is at Redlands, and its 1991 to 2020 normals show 12.87 inches of rain a year. January through March are the wettest months, while June through September each average only about 0.1 inch. With so little summer rain, a soggy lawn strip or a weeping hose bib in July is a leak, not weather. The local water company asks customers to repair leaks promptly.

Freezing is rare. The same station averages about 3 days a year at or below 32 degrees, so a ceiling stain in January usually points to a worn part or a roof problem before it points to a frozen pipe.

Riverside Highland Water Company and what its report says

The city's utilities page names Riverside Highland Water Company as the water provider for Grand Terrace. Its office number is (909) 825-4128, and its 2025 consumer report says service calls are answered 24/7 at that number. If pressure drops on the whole street, that company is the first call.

The 2025 report lists three sources. Company wells supplied 52 percent, the Baseline Feeder supplied 15 percent, and the Encanto Booster supplied 33 percent from the City of San Bernardino.

Total hardness in that report averages 193.3 ppm, with a range of 150 to 270 ppm. Scale settles in tank bottoms and faucet aerators wherever hardness is present, so a slow or noisy tank is worth a look. A water heater repair visit can start with draining and inspecting the tank. Test your tap before you buy treatment equipment.

State law also requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced against earthquake movement. The city's Safety Element places the San Jacinto Fault about 0.2 mile to the northeast, so check the straps on any tank.

A city-owned sewer that Colton maintains

According to the city's public works page, Grand Terrace owns the sewer collection system and Colton operates and maintains it. Burrtec bills residential sewer service at (909) 889-1969, and the city's main line is (909) 954-5200. The city page does not say where the private lateral begins, so ask before you assume.

A backup that shows up in every drain at once, or sewage in a yard cleanout, suggests the main or the lateral. One slow sink or tub usually means a clog in a branch line, and drain cleaning clears it. Because Colton handles the main, mention the street when you call so the right side of the problem gets checked first.

Older houses, permits and what to ask

The city's draft housing element from December 2021 says over 80 percent of the housing stock was built before 1990. Original supply lines, older toilets and decades-old drain branches are normal in that mix. Ask the plumber what pipe material they find before you agree to a repair.

The Building and Safety FAQ lists water heater replacement and re-piping among projects that need a permit, and says the list is not complete. Sewer work is not addressed, so call Building and Safety at (909) 954-5181. California also requires a contractor license on any project that needs a building permit, so check the license on the CSLB lookup.

A toilet that runs, rocks or leaks at the base is often a worn part, and toilet repair is quicker than replacing the fixture. A cracked bowl or a base leak that returns after a new seal is the point to talk about replacement.

Clay soil, slopes and storm runoff

The Safety Element describes most of the developed area as older alluvial fan and clay-rich alluvium, with groundwater around 140 feet down. It flags localized flooding at Pico Street, Vivienda Avenue, Burns Avenue and Grand Terrace Road. It also flags slope concerns on East Canal Street, Mount Vernon Avenue and Vista Grande Way.

Those are planning facts, not pipe diagnoses. If water pools after a storm near those streets, shut the taps and read the meter. A still meter means runoff, and a moving meter means a leak. Give the plumber your cross street, because hillside addresses are hard to find in the dark.

Before a plumber arrives in Grand Terrace

Call (909) 505-3225 and a licensed plumber answers 24/7. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. While you wait, work through these four steps.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the house valve clockwise until it stops. If water is pouring from the meter box on the street side, also call Riverside Highland Water Company at (909) 825-4128.

Kill power near water

If water reaches outlets, a ceiling light or the water heater wiring, switch off power at the panel only if you can reach it on a dry floor.

Clear the area and move valuables

Lift rugs and boxes off the floor, and keep children and pets away from any drain that is backing up.

Call the 24/7 line

Give the street and nearest cross street, such as Barton Road or Mount Vernon Avenue. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 first.
