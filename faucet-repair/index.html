Water spraying from a faucet or valve? Shut the house water off first

A faucet or supply valve that is spraying, snapped off or pouring water is an emergency, and the line for faucet repair is open 24/7. A faucet is a valve with a spout, fed by small shutoff valves, so a failure at either can move a lot of water fast. While you wait, shut off the main house valve, then open the lowest faucet in the house to relieve pressure.

The main valve is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house wall. Keep children and pets out of the wet room. If water is near a power strip, a garage opener or the bathroom fan, stay out and switch off that circuit only from a dry spot at the panel.

Take a quick photo of the flooding before cleanup, then mop the area and call. The line answers on nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

The shutoff valve that will not shut off, or breaks when you turn it

Under-sink stop valves and the ones behind toilets are some of the least expensive parts in a house and some of the most abused. Many sit unused for ten or twenty years. Mineral scale grows inside the stem, the packing dries, and the first time you need the valve it either spins freely, refuses to turn, or leaks around the handle the moment you touch it.

A burst or broken supply valve usually means the valve body cracked, the compression nut corroded through, or the braided supply line failed at the crimp. The fix is not to crank harder. The plumber who comes out closes the water at the main, cuts out the failed valve and installs a new quarter-turn valve, which has a ball inside and stops the water with half a turn instead of many.

If the main house valve is also stuck, say so on the call. That changes the first steps, because the water has to be stopped upstream before anything under the sink is touched.

Dripping spout, loose handle, low flow: what the faucet is telling you

Most faucet complaints trace to a small part. A cartridge, a ceramic disc, a ball assembly or a pair of rubber seats wears out, and the faucet drips from the spout or leaks around the handle. The inside of the spout can also fill with scale, which narrows the flow and makes it spray sideways from the aerator.

In Loma Linda, the city draws its water from groundwater wells in the Bunker Hill Basin, and its 2025 water quality report lists hardness values that differ by well. Ask the city utility for its annual water quality report and test your tap, because how fast aerators and cartridges scale up depends on what comes out of your own faucet.

Signs a faucet or valve needs repair

  • ✓A drip that continues after the handle is fully closed
  • ✓Water under the sink cabinet, or a swollen or stained cabinet floor
  • ✓A handle that is stiff, loose, or moves without changing the temperature
  • ✓Water pressure that drops at one faucet but not the others
  • ✓Green or white crust on the base of the faucet or at the stop valve
  • ✓Hot and cold mixing at a single-handle faucet when the water is off
  • ✓A hissing sound from a wall or cabinet when no water is running

A faucet that drips once a second is wasting water all day, and a valve with crust on the stem is likely to fail when you need it. Both are cheaper to deal with on a planned visit than during a flood. If the water bill has crept up and you cannot find a visible drip, an inside wall may be wet, which is a job for leak detection.

Shower valves, tub spouts and bathtub faucets

A shower valve sits inside the wall, so a problem there feels bigger than it is. If the water will not get hot, swings between hot and cold, or drips from the showerhead after you turn it off, the cartridge or the pressure-balance spool inside the valve is usually worn. The plumber removes the trim, shuts the water to the valve, and replaces the cartridge. Some older valves have no stops, so the main house valve must be closed first.

A bathtub faucet that leaks from the spout, or a diverter that sends water to the spout when the shower is on, is a related fix. The diverter is a small gate in the spout or a pull-up knob on top, and when it wears, you get a weak shower and a constant dribble.

If the wall is stained or soft behind the valve, or the tile is loose, water may have been leaking inside for some time. Say that on the call so the plumber can plan to check for hidden damage.

Shower and tub drains that clog or stand

A tub or shower that holds water around your ankles is almost always hair and soap packed into the first few inches of the drain, or into the trap just below. The stopper, the strainer and the overflow plate all hold hair. A plumber pulls the stopper, clears the trap with a small hand auger or a drain snake, and checks that the tub fills and empties cleanly.

If you have used a store-bought chemical cleaner, say so. The liquid can sit in the trap and burn skin or eyes when the plumber opens it. When more than one fixture is slow, or the tub gurgles when a washer drains, the blockage sits further down the line, and drain cleaning with a longer cable or a hydro jet is the better tool.

Ways the cause gets found

The plumber starts with the fixture in front of them, checks the handle action, then turns the water on and off while watching the spout, the stems and the supply lines. A flashlight and a dry paper towel under each joint show a slow seep better than any guess.

For a shower valve, the plumber confirms the pressure at the stops, then opens the trim to see the cartridge. For a drain, a mirror and a light at the strainer show whether it is hair or a collapsed trap arm. For a wall seep with no visible drip, a moisture meter is placed against the drywall around the valve, and the plumber compares readings with a dry spot nearby.

Rental homes and faucet leaks

About 62 percent of Loma Linda households rent, so many faucet calls start with a tenant who sees a leak and a property manager who has to decide what to do about it. A faucet that does not shut off, or a tub that will not drain, is not just a nuisance, because a leak that runs for days can damage the cabinet and the unit below.

Tenants should report in writing and take photos. Landlords and managers can call the line and say which unit, whether the tenant will be home, and whether the shutoff behind the fixture works.

Night and weekend calls for faucets and valves

Faucets and valves break when people are home, which means evenings and weekends. A snapped angle stop at nine at night has the same flow as one at nine in the morning. The line is open 24/7, and after-hours calls get a clear price before work starts.

For a stuck or broken shutoff valve after hours, the plumber closes the water at the main, replaces the failed piece, tests it, and restores water to the house. A slow shower valve leak can sometimes wait for a same-day visit, while a leak behind a wall that is getting worse is treated as urgent. Neighbors in Grand Terrace and Highland can call the same line.

Faucet, valve and drain questions

Scale and old packing seize the stem, especially on valves that have not moved in years. Forcing it can snap the stem or crack the body and flood the cabinet. Call, and the plumber can shut the water at the main and replace the valve with a quarter-turn one.

Replace the cartridge or washers if the faucet is otherwise sound. Replace the whole faucet if the finish is gone, the body is pitted, or parts are no longer made. A leaking base on a corroded body usually means replacement.
