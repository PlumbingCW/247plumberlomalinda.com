How to Shut Off the Main Water Valve at Your Loma Linda, CA Home

The main shut-off valve stops all water to the house, and knowing its type and location before a pipe bursts saves floors, walls, and stress.

By the Loma Linda 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-23Updated 2026-10-23
Call (909) 505-3225

How to turn off the water to the whole house

The main shut-off valve controls all the water entering your home. Closing it stops a burst pipe, a failed supply line, or a flooding water heater at the source. Every household member should know where it is and how it turns.

  1. 1Find the main valve. It is usually on the line where water enters the house, often near a wall, in a garage, a utility closet, or outside near the meter side of the house.
  2. 2Identify the type. A round wheel handle is usually a gate valve. A lever handle is usually a ball valve.
  3. 3For a wheel, turn it clockwise until it stops. For a lever, turn it a quarter turn so the handle sits crosswise to the pipe.
  4. 4Open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to confirm the flow stops. A trickle that dies out is normal.
  5. 5Set the water heater to off or pilot if you plan to drain the lines, so the tank does not heat while empty of supply.

Remember the rule, righty tighty, lefty loosey. Turn gently. Never force a valve with a wrench or a pipe on the handle.

Gate valves versus ball valves

Two designs are common, and they behave differently. A gate valve uses a wheel that raises and lowers a flat gate inside the valve. It takes several full turns to close. Over time, mineral buildup and corrosion can make it stiff, and a gate can break off its stem so the handle spins without closing anything.

A ball valve uses a lever and a ball with a hole through it. It closes with a quarter turn, which is faster and less likely to seize. Many homeowners prefer them, and a plumber can swap a worn gate valve for a ball valve.

Where valves are usually found, and where not to assume

Homes differ, so check yours rather than guess. In general, a main valve sits where the water line enters the building. People find them in garages, on the outside of a wall, in a utility closet, or near the water heater. A second valve sits at the meter, and that one is not the homeowner's.

Do not assume the layout from a neighbor's house. A previous remodel may have moved the valve. Walk the property, look for the line coming through the wall, and trace it.

Tag the valve with a bright label once you find it. Take a photo and share it with everyone in the home, including renters and house sitters.

Loma Linda local note: your valve and the city's meter

Water service here is municipal, run by the City of Loma Linda Water Department, with its own meters and connections. The meter belongs to the utility, so the valve at the meter is part of the utility's equipment. Before you operate anything at the meter box, ask the city where its part ends and yours starts.

If water is running on the street side, for example a leak coming up from a meter box or a main break, the after-hours emergency number for water, sewer, and street problems is (909) 356-3805. Use it for problems outside your property. For a pipe inside the house, your own main valve is the quick fix.

The city pages do not tell residents where main valves sit in Loma Linda homes, so no assumption is made here. Check your own house.

Why a valve sticks and what to do

A valve that has not moved in years can stick. Mineral deposits harden around the stem. In a city where hardness varies by well, scale is a plausible cause, and a test of your own tap shows where you stand. A seized valve also hides damage inside.

Try this: apply steady, gentle pressure, then back off. Do not use a pipe wrench or a long cheater bar, because gate valves crack, and a cracked valve can flood the house. Do not hammer it.

If the valve will not turn, will not seal, or leaks around the stem, stop. Call a plumber. Meanwhile, close any smaller shut-off valves under sinks and behind toilets, which can isolate a single fixture. Turning off the water heater and power to it can help if the heater itself is leaking.

Before the emergency happens

Spend ten minutes a year on the valve. Operate it gently from open to closed and back, and look for drips around the stem. If the handle is hard to move or leaks, schedule a replacement while nothing is flooding.

Know where your fixture valves are too. Under each sink and behind each toilet, a small valve shuts off one fixture. Learning them lets you stop a single leak without losing water to the whole house.

Check that the family knows. A valve that only one person can find does not help at 2 a.m. For multi-person households, practice once.

When a homeowner should call a plumber

Some jobs are not for homeowners. Replacing a main shut-off valve means working on the pressurized line, and it often involves the point near the meter. Licensed work is the rule when a job needs a permit or passes the state's cost threshold. The Contractors State License Board lookup lets you verify a plumber before the job.

Also call if water will not stop after the valve is closed. A leak that continues may be upstream, on the utility side, or a sign of a crack beyond the valve. A plumber using leak detection tools can find a hidden break when the source is not obvious, and a burst pipe repair fixes the failure once it is located.

Neighboring city notes

Utilities set different boundaries. Over in San Bernardino, the municipal water department says in its FAQ that it maintains the main up to the meter and that the customer owns the buried line beyond it. That is San Bernardino's rule only.

Colton posts its own after-hours water number for emergencies. If you live near a city line, check your bill to see which utility serves you before you call the wrong one.

Cost drivers and calling in an emergency

A valve replacement or burst repair depends on several factors.

  • Pipe material and the size of the line.
  • How accessible the valve and pipe are.
  • Whether the water must be shut off at the meter.
  • Permit needs, which vary by city.
  • Whether the call comes at night, on a weekend, or on a holiday.

Water damage from a sudden break is treated differently by insurers than damage from a slow drip, so ask yours and keep photos and receipts. If water is flooding now, shut the main, then call.
