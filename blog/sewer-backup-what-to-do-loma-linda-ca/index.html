Sewer Backup in Loma Linda, CA: What to Do in the First Hour

When sewage rises in a tub or floor drain, stop all water use, keep people away, and find out whether the blockage is yours or the city's.

By the Loma Linda 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-23Updated 2026-10-23
Call (909) 505-3225

First steps when sewage is coming up

A sewer backup means wastewater is flowing back up through your drains instead of out to the street. Treat it as an emergency, because sewage carries bacteria and the longer it sits, the more it spreads. These steps come first, in this order.

  1. 1Stop using water. No flushing, no showers, no laundry, no dishwasher. Every gallon you send down adds to the backup.
  2. 2Keep children, pets, and anyone with health concerns out of the wet area.
  3. 3Do not touch sewage with bare hands. Wear rubber gloves and boots if you must go in.
  4. 4If water has reached outlets, appliances, or a cord, stay out. Shut power at the panel only if you can reach it with dry hands and a dry floor.
  5. 5Open windows for air if the weather allows, and leave the room if the smell is strong.
  6. 6Photograph the damage before moving anything.

Do not pour chemical openers into a backed-up line, and do not use a plunger on a full tub or floor drain. Both can spray or push sewage around.

Is it your line or the city main?

This question decides who you call first. If only your house is backing up, the blockage is probably in your own sewer line. If neighbors on the street are also backing up, the problem may be in the public main, which the city handles.

You can often tell by the pattern. A backup in the lowest drain of the house, such as a shower or floor drain, when you run water upstairs points to the line leaving your house. Ask the people next door if their drains are slow too. Many main-line problems show up on several homes at once.

Do not decide by guess alone. When in doubt, make both calls. A plumber can camera your line and see whether the blockage sits on your side, and the city can check the main.

Loma Linda local note: the city owns the collection system

Loma Linda's city government operates the sewer collection network, so a blockage in the public main under the street is the city's to handle. The after-hours number for water, sewer, and street emergencies is (909) 356-3805. Call it if you suspect the main, or if several homes on your street are affected.

Ownership of the private lateral, the pipe from your house toward the main, is not stated on the city pages used for this article. Put that question to the city instead of assuming. This matters because a plumber working in a public street or right-of-way may need to coordinate with the city, and the right party to fix a collapse depends on where it sits.

Wastewater from Loma Linda is treated at San Bernardino's plant under a joint powers agreement, so the city line, not a treatment plant, is your point of contact.

What a plumber does at a backup

A plumber starts by finding the cleanout and relieving the pressure. A cable machine or hydro jet clears the blockage, and then a camera confirms the pipe is open and shows the cause. Roots, grease, a belly in the pipe, or a crack are common.

If the camera finds a damaged section, the plumber explains the repair options, which may include spot repair or replacement. Our page on sewer line cleaning explains the clearing side, and sewer line repair explains what happens when a pipe is broken.

Cleanup is a separate step. Sewage-soaked drywall, carpet, and padding often need to be removed, and a restoration company usually handles that. A plumber stops the source, but cleanup of porous materials is a different trade.

Neighbors in nearby cities

Utilities handle backups differently. In Redlands, the utility lists a weekday number and an after-hours number for backups, and says a city crew checks the main first. If the main is clear, the city may advise calling a plumber for a lateral blockage. That is Redlands' process, not Loma Linda's.

Grand Terrace sends its wastewater to the Colton plant, according to Colton's own utility page. If you live in Grand Terrace or near a city line, check which agency serves your street. We serve Loma Linda and nearby towns, and the first question is always who owns the pipe.

What not to do, and the health side

Do not run a rented cable machine into a full line. Do not try to clean up sewage with a household vacuum, which spreads bacteria through the air. Do not let children or pets walk through affected rooms.

Wash hands with soap and water after any contact. If anyone develops stomach illness or skin irritation after exposure, contact a doctor. Dry affected areas quickly, and discard porous items that were soaked in sewage.

Replacing a sewer line is work for a licensed contractor, so look the plumber up on the state license board site. Permit rules for digging in the street are the city's to state, and the Building and Safety Division is the place to ask.

After the line is open

Once the line is clear, ask what caused it. A grease buildup means changing kitchen habits. Roots mean the pipe has an entry point, and clearing them only buys time until the joint is fixed. Wipes, even those sold as flushable, belong in the trash. A camera recording from the visit is worth keeping, because it documents the pipe condition for an insurer or a landlord.

If the backup happened once and the camera shows a clean, sound pipe, a one-time cause such as a toy or a heavy grease slug is likely. If it happens again, treat the line as a problem to fix, not a chore to repeat.

Cost drivers, insurance, and when to call

Several things shape the price of a backup call.

  • How far the blockage is from the cleanout.
  • The cause: grease, roots, a broken pipe, or a foreign object.
  • Pipe material and depth.
  • Whether a camera pass and a follow-up repair are needed.
  • Night, weekend, or holiday timing of the call.
