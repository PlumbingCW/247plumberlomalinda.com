What these guides are

These guides are written for people who live in Loma Linda and the towns around it. Each one starts with the direct answer, then explains the steps, what you can safely do yourself and the point where you should stop and call a licensed plumber.

They are not repair manuals for work that needs a license or a permit. A guide can help you read a symptom, shut off the right valve and ask the right questions. It does not replace a plumber standing in your house.

What each guide covers

Topics follow the calls a Loma Linda plumber hears most often: drains that clog again, water heaters that stop heating or leak, meters that keep moving, and fixtures that run or drip. Each guide names the tools a plumber uses, such as a sewer camera or a hydro jet, so you know what a visit involves.

Each guide also has a short Loma Linda note, built from city and government sources, and a FAQ for the follow-up questions people ask next.

Safety comes first

Every guide puts safety ahead of savings. If you smell gas, the answer is always to leave the building and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Water near electrical outlets means staying out of the room. Power at the panel is switched off only if you can reach it with dry hands and a dry floor.

Where a guide mentions Loma Linda facts, such as the age of local homes or how the city handles water and sewer, those facts come from city and government sources. When a rule varies, the guide tells you who to ask, such as the Loma Linda Building and Safety Division.
