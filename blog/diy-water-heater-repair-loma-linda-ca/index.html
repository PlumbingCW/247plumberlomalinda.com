DIY Water Heater Repair in Loma Linda, CA: What Is Safe to Try

Some water heater problems have a simple cause you can check in minutes. Others involve gas, power or a leaking tank. Here is where Loma Linda homeowners should draw the line.

By the Loma Linda 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-23Updated 2026-10-23
Call (909) 505-3225

What you can safely try and what you cannot

Do it yourself water heater repair is safe for a short list of checks: the power or pilot, the temperature setting, a tripped breaker, and a visible drip at a fitting you can tighten. Anything involving a gas line, the burner, the relief valve, wiring, or a leaking tank is for a licensed plumber.

The reason is simple. A water heater combines gas or high-amp power, scalding water and a heavy steel tank, and mistakes with any of the three hurt people. The rest of this page sorts the common failures by what is within a homeowner's reach.

The base leak row matters most. If the tank is leaking, no repair will hold, and the better path is replacement, which is covered under water heater replacement.

Gas first: what to do if you smell it

If you smell gas near the heater, do not light anything, do not flip switches and do not try to find the leak. Leave the house right away, and from outside call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200. Call 911 if anyone is in danger or you cannot reach SoCalGas. Do not call a plumber first.

If there is no gas smell, a homeowner may relight a standing pilot by following the label on the unit exactly. If the pilot goes out again within a day, stop. A failing thermocouple or a draft problem needs a licensed plumber, since the cause could be unsafe venting.

Electric water heaters and the danger of wet wiring

Electric heaters add a different hazard. The elements and thermostats sit behind access panels, and the wires carry household power. Turn the breaker off at the panel before you open anything, and confirm the power is off with a tester.

If the floor around the heater is wet, do not step into it. Water and live power are a deadly pair. Switch off the breaker only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry surface, and if you cannot, stay back and call for help. A homeowner should never replace a heating element or a thermostat on a wet or flooded unit.

Simple maintenance a homeowner can handle

Some upkeep helps a heater last longer and carries limited risk.

  • Set the thermostat to the maker's suggested temperature, usually printed on the label.
  • Look for rust, moisture or mineral crust at the pipe connections once a season.
  • Check the earthquake straps. State law requires water heaters to be braced against falling or sliding in an earthquake, so loose straps are worth fixing.
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve into a bucket or a hose run outside to release sediment, with the power or gas set to off or pilot, following the maker's directions.

Skip the temperature and pressure relief valve. It protects the tank from over-pressure, and it can fail to reseat after a test and begin to drip. Replacing it, or fixing the discharge pipe, is work for a plumber.

Parts that look easy but are not

Replacing an anode rod, a gas control valve, a burner assembly or a vent connection looks like a swap. Each affects safety in a way that is hard to see. A bad vent connection can let exhaust into the house. A wrong gas fitting can leak slowly and quietly. If a repair involves cutting, re-gluing or re-sealing a gas or vent joint, put the tools down.

When a repair keeps recurring, the cost question changes. A heater that needs repeated parts may be near the end of its life, and a water heater repair quote next to a replacement quote helps you decide with real numbers in front of you.

Loma Linda local note: gas, permits and licence checks

SoCalGas is the regional gas utility, and its published leak guidance is the same everywhere in its territory: leave, call from a safe place, and use 911 when needed. Keep that number, 1-800-427-2200, with the water heater information on your phone so you do not search for it during an emergency.

The Loma Linda Building and Safety Division states that plumbing work needs its own permit and that a licensed contractor or the owner must obtain it. The city pages do not name water heater replacement or repair, so permit rules vary, and the division is the place to ask before you replace a unit. Gas and electrical work on a heater may each fall under separate rules.

Before you hire help, use the California Contractors State License Board lookup. It lets you enter a licence number or a business name and shows status and any complaint disclosure. The board says a licence is required when a project passes its cost threshold, and for any job that needs a building permit, so replacing a heater with permit-level work generally calls for a licensed contractor.

When to stop and call

Stop if you smell gas, see rust-colored water leaking from the tank, hear a hiss from the relief valve, find a wet floor around an electric unit, or have already reset a breaker twice. Shut off the cold water supply to the heater if it is leaking, and keep people away from the area.
