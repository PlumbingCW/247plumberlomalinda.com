DIY Tankless Water Heater: A Loma Linda, CA Homeowner Guide

A tankless heater promises endless hot water, but the install touches gas, venting and power. Here is what a Loma Linda homeowner can do and what needs a licensed pro.

By the Loma Linda 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished 2026-10-23Updated 2026-10-23
Call (909) 505-3225

The honest answer on installing a tankless heater yourself

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap is open, using a burner or electric elements. The install is rarely a DIY job. It involves a gas line often larger than the one feeding a tank, a vent made of the right material, a drain for condensation on high-efficiency models, and in many homes a new electric supply.

What a homeowner can do is plan, pick the unit with a pro's help, prepare the space, and handle maintenance after the install. The core connections and the gas work belong with a licensed plumber, and the next sections explain why.

Why a tankless unit is a bigger job than a tank swap

A tank heater plugs into the home's existing lines and sits on a flat pad. A tankless unit hangs on a wall and asks more of the house. It draws a burst of gas when it fires, so the gas pipe must carry enough. It needs a vent path suited to the model, which may mean new pipe through a wall or roof. Many units also need power for the controls and a path for acid condensate.

Because of that, a swap from a tank to a tankless can turn into several jobs at once: gas, vent, electric, water and sometimes drywall. Each has its own failure points, and a mistake in any one can be dangerous.

If you plan to do any part yourself, treat the gas and vent rows as hard stops. No amount of YouTube replaces training on gas piping.

Gas safety, in plain steps

Gas is the part where mistakes show up late. A joint that is slightly off can leak for weeks.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and do not use switches, phones or lighters inside. From a safe place outside, call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200. If you cannot reach them, or anyone is in danger, call 911. Plumbers repair customer-side gas piping and appliances, but a gas smell goes to the utility first.

Do not test a gas joint with a flame. Do not guess at a fitting. Do not turn a gas valve on after work you did yourself unless a licensed professional has pressure-tested the line.

Loma Linda local note: air rules, permits and licences

Loma Linda lies inside the South Coast Air Quality Management District. Its Rule 1121 covers residential-type natural gas water heaters under 75,000 Btu per hour of heat input, and it requires low-NOx models. The rule text says units at or above that input are exempt, so the rating on the plate matters. Ask the plumber to confirm that the model you choose complies, and check the district website for its list of compliant models before buying.

The district board voted down a plan in June 2025 that would have required zero-NOx water heaters. No such rule has been adopted, so a compliant gas tankless unit remains an option. Rules can change, and the district is the source to check.

On permits, the Loma Linda Building and Safety Division says plumbing, electrical and mechanical work each need a separate permit, and that a licensed contractor or the owner must obtain it. The city pages do not name tankless installs, so permit rules vary, and a call to the division is the next step. Inspection is required before pipes buried beneath grade are covered, which matters if the gas line has to be run.

The state licence board requires a contractor licence once a project passes its cost threshold, or whenever a project needs a building permit. A tankless install with new gas and vent work will usually meet one of those tests. Check any contractor on the board's lookup before you hire.

What a homeowner should do before calling

You can help a plumber give you a clear answer by gathering information.

  • Photograph the existing heater label, the gas meter and the gas line leading to the heater.
  • Note how many bathrooms, showers and appliances run at once in a typical morning.
  • Measure the wall space and check whether an exterior wall is close for venting.
  • Ask the gas utility about incentives, because SoCalGas has listed rebates for high-efficiency gas storage and tankless heaters, funded first come, first served through 2026 and subject to change.
  • Check whether a heat pump water heater could be an option instead, and check whether state or utility incentives are still open, since those funds have been fully reserved in the past.

Skip any plan that involves cutting into a flue or a roof by yourself. That is where roof leaks and carbon monoxide risks begin.

Maintenance you can do yourself

After a pro installs the unit, the most useful DIY task is a flush with the maker's isolation valves to clear scale. The maker's manual describes the steps, the pump, the cleaning liquid and the time. Turn the power and gas control to the settings the manual calls for, and run the flush in a well-ventilated area.

A good rule is to keep the manual and the install date together on a note on the wall. If an error code shows, look it up in the manual before resetting anything, and call a plumber if the code involves ignition, venting or flame failure. The answer is almost always that you can prepare and maintain, and a licensed plumber should do the gas and vent. We serve both towns.

When to call a plumber

Call before you buy a unit if you are unsure about gas line size, vent routing or power. Call right away if you smell gas after any work, see soot near the vent, or notice error codes that mention ignition or flame. A tank swap or tankless install does not have to wait for office hours, because the line is open 24/7, including nights and weekends.
